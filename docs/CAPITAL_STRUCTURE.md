# Capital structure, governance and risk — AGI Future Foundation PBC

*Canonical copy. Companion to [`INVESTOR_BRIEF.md`](INVESTOR_BRIEF.md); the designed edition of both is published as one data-room page. Nothing here is legal, tax or securities advice; every structure described is a proposal until counsel has papered it.*

You should frame risk factors as evidence of **disciplined governance**, not as a defensive disclaimer, and structure capital so each investor understands exactly what entity they own, what their money funds, who controls decisions, and where their economic returns come from.

For AGI Future Foundation PBC, AGI Corp, Cognition.X, Robotics.X, and Locator.X, the essential rule is: **do not mix mission, software, property, robotics hardware, and investor economics inside an unclear entity structure.** Separate each capital pool, define its rights, and disclose the risks plainly.

## Core investor framing

Use this opening language in an investor memo or data room:

> **AGI is building mission-governed commercial platforms for AI readiness, workforce training, simulation, robotics, and real-estate intelligence. Our capital structure is designed to separate operating-company risk, property-asset risk, and public-benefit governance while preserving a shared technology and mission advantage.**
>
> **Investors receive economic exposure only to the entity and assets identified in their financing documents. AGI does not commingle capital across operating subsidiaries, property vehicles, grant-funded programs, or public-benefit initiatives without explicit legal authority, board approval, and investor disclosure.**

That statement immediately addresses the biggest diligence question: "What am I buying?"

### The investor promise

Investors should hear five clear commitments:

1. **Clear entity ownership:** Every investment instrument names the exact issuer.
2. **Ring-fenced use of proceeds:** Capital is used only for the approved budget of that issuer or SPV.
3. **Defined governance:** Board composition, voting rights, founder rights, mission rights, investor protections, and conflicts procedures are documented.
4. **Honest risk disclosure:** The company describes material risks without claiming guaranteed outcomes.
5. **Mission with commercial discipline:** PBC commitments are protected, but they do not create ambiguity around commercial authority or investor economics.

Private placements can be illiquid, high-risk investments, including the possibility of complete loss; investors should understand that securities may be restricted and difficult to resell ([investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/private)).

## Recommended capital structure

### Separate the capital pools

Use a **parent-plus-subsidiary structure** with distinct operating and asset vehicles.

| Entity | Capital purpose | What an investor owns | Use of proceeds | What investors do not own automatically |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AGI Future Foundation PBC** | Mission governance, public-benefit initiatives, fellowships, standards, research, scholarships | PBC equity or mission-aligned economic interest, if it raises capital directly | Public-benefit programs, governance, research, impact reporting, approved shared services | Automatic direct interest in AGI Corp, Cognition.X, Locator.X, Robotics.X, or property SPVs unless expressly documented |
| **AGI Corp** | Parent operating company and shared IP platform | Equity or SAFE/convertible instrument in AGI Corp | Core IP, platform infrastructure, AI/data systems, shared technical teams, security, legal, central operations | Direct ownership of real-estate assets unless AGI Corp owns the relevant SPV interests |
| **Cognition.X, Inc. or LLC** | Education, training, simulations, content, and enterprise learning platform | Parent-company equity or direct subsidiary equity, depending on the round | Learning platform, curriculum, simulation content, customer success, enterprise pilots, integrations | Direct interest in Locator.X, property assets, or unrelated AGI commercial lines |
| **Robotics.X / AGI Robotics Lab** | Robotics simulation, lab partnerships, hardware pilots, workforce training | AGI Corp equity, dedicated subsidiary equity, equipment financing, grant participation, or project financing | Robotics curriculum, simulation environments, leased equipment, labs, integrations, safety programs | Direct ownership of unrelated software or real estate unless expressly included |
| **Locator.X** | Real-estate intelligence, underwriting, acquisition workflow, portfolio analytics | AGI Corp equity or dedicated Locator.X equity | Product, data licenses, AI workflows, go-to-market, integrations, customer acquisition | Direct economic interest in individual properties unless the investor also invests in a property vehicle |
| **AGI Property Holdings / asset SPV** | Acquisition, renovation, development, operation, refinance, or sale of defined real estate | Membership interest, preferred equity, debt note, or other property-specific instrument | Purchase price, closing costs, rehab, reserves, debt service, operating expenses | Equity in AGI Corp, Cognition.X, Robotics.X, or Locator.X unless expressly offered |

### The most important clarification

Do **not** pitch one investment as funding everything.

Avoid:

> "Invest in AGI and receive exposure to our AI, training, robotics, real estate, and foundation portfolio."

Use:

> "This offering is issued by [exact entity]. Proceeds will be used solely for [approved budget]. The investor receives [exact security] in [exact entity]. Any ownership, license, revenue-sharing, or shared-services relationship with AGI-affiliated entities is described in the transaction documents."

This protects both investors and AGI from confusion, conflicts, and future litigation risk.

A practical structure would be:

```text
├── Publishes impact reporting

     │

├── Locator.X (real-estate intelligence and investor operating system)

      │
      ├── AGI Property SPV 001, LLC
      ├── AGI Property SPV 002, LLC
      └── AGI Property SPV 003, LLC
```

### Mission-protection rights

The PBC should ideally have **limited protective rights**, rather than unlimited operating control. Delaware PBC directors are required to balance stockholder economic interests, interests of materially affected stakeholders, and the specific public benefit set out in the charter ([8 Del. C. §362 et seq.](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc15/)).

Use a reserved-matters schedule under which Foundation approval is required only for actions such as:

- Amending or removing the defined public-benefit purpose.
- Selling or exclusively licensing mission-critical AGI safety/governance IP.
- Dissolving the PBC or materially abandoning its mission commitments.
- Removing core AI safety, accessibility, privacy, or human-oversight policies.
- Selling the company in a transaction that materially conflicts with the PBC mission.
- Changing the mission council structure or impact-reporting obligations.
- Entering a related-party transaction above a defined materiality threshold.

Do **not** give the PBC broad approval authority over:

- Normal hiring and firing.
- Pricing decisions.
- Routine product roadmap decisions.
- Enterprise sales contracts.
- Day-to-day budgets.
- Financing under a preapproved board plan.
- Standard vendor selection.

That balance preserves mission integrity without making the commercial company unfinanceable.

## Financing instruments

### Best instrument by stage

| Stage | Recommended instrument | Why it fits | Key cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friends, family, early angels | **Post-money SAFE** issued by AGI Corp | Simple, relatively fast, no maturity date or interest, defers valuation | Model dilution carefully; use one standardized form where possible; do not stack uncontrolled SAFE caps |
| Pre-seed with sophisticated angels | **Post-money SAFE with valuation cap and pro rata side letter** | Preserves simplicity while offering clearer future participation rights | Avoid excessive MFN, discount, cap, and special-right combinations |
| Seed round | **Preferred stock** in AGI Corp or chosen commercial subsidiary | Market-standard for institutional investors; sets governance and liquidation terms clearly | Requires more legal work, board approvals, charter amendments, and a full financing package |
| Customer pilots | **Paid pilot / implementation agreement** | Non-dilutive validation and revenue | Avoid custom work that distracts from product strategy; define IP ownership |
| Grants, philanthropy, workforce funding | **Restricted grant or program contract** | Non-dilutive capital for mission-aligned training, access, research, or workforce programs | Track restricted funds separately; honor reporting, allowable-cost, and IP conditions |
| Robotics equipment | **Lease, equipment finance, OEM partnership, or customer-funded lab** | Limits upfront hardware capex | Watch debt covenants, personal guarantees, maintenance obligations, and utilization risk |
| Individual property acquisition | **SPV equity, preferred equity, secured debt, or lender financing** | Keeps real-estate economics and risks tied to the asset | Requires securities, real-estate, tax, lending, and investor-disclosure work |
| Larger commercial rollout | **Venture debt or revenue-based financing, later** | Can preserve equity once recurring revenue is predictable | Do not use too early; repayment and covenants can strain a pre-revenue company |

A SAFE is an agreement under which the company promises a future ownership interest upon a triggering event such as a future equity financing or acquisition. Unlike convertible notes, SAFEs generally do not have a maturity date, do not accrue interest, and do not create creditor rights ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/common-startup-securities)).

## Risk factors

### How to present risk

Use a three-layer approach:

1. **Investor deck:** Brief, balanced, non-alarmist risk summary.
2. **Investor memo / data room:** Detailed risk register with mitigation plan and ownership.
3. **Legal offering materials:** Counsel-reviewed risk factors, conflicts, disclosures, and subscription documentation.

Do not bury material risks. Under Rule 506(b), if non-accredited investors participate, issuers must provide specified disclosure and financial information and make available information given to accredited investors; Rule 506(b) also prohibits general solicitation. Most venture financings are structured as equity and must fit within an available securities offering exemption, commonly Regulation D for accredited investors ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/private-placements-rule-506b)).

### Investor-deck risk language

Use this concise paragraph:

> **Risk management:** AGI operates in early-stage, regulated, and technically evolving markets. Key risks include product execution, AI-model reliability, customer adoption, long enterprise sales cycles, data privacy and security, robotics safety, capital availability, and mission-governance complexity. We mitigate these risks through a phased product roadmap, simulation-first robotics strategy, human oversight, standards-based interoperability, separate legal entities and capital pools, rigorous data controls, customer-funded pilots, and board-level governance.

### Full risk register

| Risk category | Specific risk | Why it matters | Mitigation / investor response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early-stage execution** | Products may take longer or cost more to build than expected | Delays revenue, increases burn, may require additional capital | Stage-gated roadmap; narrow initial use cases; quarterly milestone review; maintain 12–18 months runway; prioritize paid pilots |
| **Market adoption** | Customers may not adopt AI training, simulation, robotics, or investor platforms at anticipated pace | Reduces revenue and delays product-market fit | Start with urgent use cases; sell pilots; use measurable outcomes; focus on sectors with recurring training/compliance needs |
| **Enterprise sales cycle** | Procurement, security review, legal review, and integrations can delay contracts | Revenue timing becomes unpredictable | Maintain pilot offer; build procurement package early; target a balanced mix of mid-market and institutional buyers |
| **Product differentiation** | Incumbent LMS, AI, simulation, robotics, CRM, or property-data vendors may copy features | Competitive pressure, lower pricing power | Focus on integrated evidence-based workflows, proprietary content, data models, partner ecosystem, and actual-performance learning loops |
| **AI quality and hallucination** | Generative AI may give incorrect, biased, unsafe, or misleading guidance | Particularly serious in education, governance, cybersecurity, and robotics contexts | Retrieval from approved sources; constrained agents; human review; model evaluations; monitoring; appeal/correction process; no AI-only high-stakes decisions |
| **AI governance and regulation** | AI laws, procurement rules, model-provider policies, and sector standards may change | Can increase compliance cost or limit deployment | NIST AI RMF-based governance; data minimization; configurable policies; legal review; model-provider diversification |
| **Data privacy** | Learner, customer, worker, property, or financial data could be mishandled or breached | Legal liability, lost trust, contract loss | Encryption, RBAC, MFA, tenant separation, DPA, retention policies, vendor review, incident response, privacy-by-design |
| **Cybersecurity** | SaaS platforms, AI agents, integrations, APIs, and robotics systems are attack targets | Breaches, ransomware, unsafe control actions, reputational damage | Secure SDLC, penetration tests, logging, secrets management, least privilege, network segmentation, incident response, third-party assessment roadmap |
| **Robotics safety** | Users may misuse hardware, training may be insufficient, or systems may malfunction | Physical injury, property damage, liability, insurance exposure | Simulation-first training; site-specific SOPs; manufacturer guidance; qualified supervision; emergency procedures; insurance; no unauthorized operational certification claims |
| **Hardware and supply chain** | Robotics hardware can be costly, unavailable, incompatible, or quickly obsolete | Capex, deployment delays, maintenance cost | Hardware-agnostic platform; leasing/partner labs; OEM integration; avoid large inventory early; customer-funded equipment where possible |
| **Training efficacy** | Learners may complete modules without improved job performance | Weak renewals and insufficient customer ROI | Baseline and post-training assessment; practical scenarios; supervisor validation; outcomes study; content iteration; avoid unsupported efficacy claims |
| **Content quality** | Content may become outdated or fail to meet domain requirements | Safety, trust, and procurement risk | SME review board; versioning; source citations; scheduled review cycles; customer feedback and change management |
| **Interoperability** | SCORM, xAPI, cmi5, LTI, identity, HRIS, LMS, or hardware integrations may fail or be expensive | Delayed enterprise deployment and increased service costs | API-first design; phased standards support; integration testing; certified partners where justified; standard implementation scope |
| **Accessibility** | Learning and simulation experiences may not meet accessibility needs | Exclusion, legal risk, lost contracts | WCAG 2.2 AA roadmap; testing; captions; keyboard access; screen-reader design; non-XR alternatives; accessibility review |
| **Property-data quality** | Locator.X may rely on incomplete, stale, licensed, or inconsistent property data | Bad underwriting, customer dissatisfaction, legal claims | Source provenance, data freshness labels, confidence scoring, multi-source verification, user review, contract controls |
| **Real-estate market risk** | Property values, rents, interest rates, liquidity, insurance costs, construction costs, or regulations may change | Direct impact on property SPV investors and portfolio economics | Separate SPVs; conservative underwriting; stress tests; reserves; independent appraisals/inspections; avoid cross-collateralization without disclosure |
| **Capital availability** | Future capital may not be available or may be highly dilutive | May constrain growth or force restructuring | Runway discipline; milestone-based fundraising; customer revenue; grants; strategic partners; avoid premature fixed repayment obligations |
| **Dilution** | SAFEs, options, warrants, and future rounds can materially dilute earlier investors and founders | Investors may misunderstand ownership outcomes | Maintain fully diluted cap table; scenario model every financing; disclose SAFE stack; adopt an equity incentive reserve deliberately |
| **Illiquidity** | Private securities may not be easily sold or transferred | Investor capital could be tied up indefinitely | Explicit disclosure; transfer restrictions; no implied liquidity promise; realistic exit discussion |
| **Founder/key-person** | Early company may depend heavily on founders or a small technical/content team | Loss or underperformance could disrupt execution | Vesting, IP assignment, hiring plan, advisor bench, documented operating procedures, key-person insurance when appropriate |
| **Governance conflict** | PBC mission rights may conflict with investor preferences or acquisition opportunities | Can complicate exits, fundraising, and board decisions | Narrowly define mission rights; independent board members; reserved matters; conflict policy; transparent impact reporting |
| **Related-party transactions** | AGI entities may license IP, share employees, or contract with each other | Potential conflicts, hidden economics, investor distrust | Written intercompany agreements; arm's-length pricing; board approval; independent review for material transactions; disclosure |
| **Regulatory and securities compliance** | Improper fundraising, marketing, or investor communications can create liability | Serious enforcement and rescission risk | Securities counsel; correct offering exemption; accredited-investor process where needed; Form D/state filings; controlled communications |
| **Reputation and public trust** | AI, robotics, and "AGI" claims can attract scrutiny | Trust loss can affect customers, partners, and investors | Avoid AGI hype; publish safety practices; transparent impact reporting; controlled claims; rapid incident communication plan |

## Capital-structure terms investors expect

### Common investor protections

At a priced seed round, sophisticated investors may expect some version of the following:

| Term | Investor rationale | Founder-friendly approach |
|---|---|---|
| **1x non-participating liquidation preference** | Investor receives money back before common stock if the company exits below expectations | Standard and generally acceptable; avoid participating preferred where possible |
| **Pro rata right** | Investor can maintain ownership in future rounds | Offer to major investors; define a threshold for eligibility |
| **Information rights** | Investor receives financial and operating updates | Quarterly reporting for major investors; protect confidential customer/data information |
| **Board observer or seat** | Investor wants visibility and governance influence | Start with observer rights or one independent/lead-investor seat; avoid oversized boards |
| **Protective provisions** | Investor consent for extraordinary actions | Limit to major items: charter changes, senior securities, sale, liquidation, large debt, option-pool expansion |
| **Founder vesting** | Ensures founders remain committed | Standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff; credit previously earned service where appropriate |
| **Option pool** | Enables hiring | Model dilution before agreeing; use a reasonable initial pool based on 12–18-month hiring needs |
| **Right of first refusal / co-sale** | Helps manage ownership transfers | Standard but should not block legitimate founder liquidity without reason |
| **Drag-along** | Enables a sale approved by required parties | Include protective thresholds and ensure PBC mission considerations are addressed in charter documents |

### PBC-specific investor protections

Add these to reduce ambiguity:

- A written **Mission Rights Agreement** defining the Foundation's narrow reserved matters.
- A **Conflict-of-Interest Policy** covering AGI Corp, the PBC, subsidiaries, founders, advisors, and property SPVs.
- A **Shared Services Agreement** documenting IP licenses, staff allocations, cost sharing, data rights, and transfer pricing.
- A **Public Benefit Reporting Policy** with annual or biennial impact metrics.
- An **Independent Director Policy** requiring at least one independent board member by the first priced round or a defined revenue threshold.
- A **Mission-Safe Exit Protocol** explaining how mission rights are handled in a merger, asset sale, or change of control.

Delaware law requires a PBC board to balance stockholder economic interests, interests of materially affected stakeholders, and the chartered public benefit. It also requires a PBC to provide stockholders at least biennially with a statement of objectives, standards, factual progress information, and assessment of success in promoting the public benefit ([8 Del. C. §366](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc15/)).

## Cap-table guidance

### Suggested pre-seed capitalization target

This is an illustrative planning model, not legal or tax advice.

| Ownership category | Target before pre-seed | Target after a $2M–$2.5M pre-seed | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Founders and core early team | 80%–90% | 60%–75% | Preserve enough ownership for long-term incentives and future rounds |
| Employee option pool | 10%–15% | 10%–15% | Reserve only what you need for the next 12–18 months of hiring |
| Pre-seed investors / SAFE holders | 0%–10% | 15%–25% | Depends on valuation cap, discount, SAFE stack, and round amount |
| Advisors | 0%–3% | 1%–3% | Use restricted, vesting-based grants; do not over-allocate |
| Foundation mission rights | Often non-economic or small economic stake | Defined by charter/agreements | Use governance rights carefully; do not create a hidden economic overhang |
| Future seed reserve / dilution | Not issued | Plan for 15%–25% additional dilution | Model this before setting early terms |

### Avoid common cap-table mistakes

- Issuing multiple SAFEs with inconsistent caps, discounts, MFN clauses, side letters, and pro-rata rights.
- Offering equity to advisors without vesting, deliverables, or IP assignment.
- Giving away an excessively large option pool before knowing the hiring plan.
- Failing to model conversion of all SAFEs, notes, options, warrants, and founder shares on a fully diluted basis.
- Mixing PBC mission voting rights with vague economic rights.
- Giving property investors an unclear claim on AGI Corp's operating-company IP.
- Cross-collateralizing real-estate SPVs with the operating company without explicit board and investor approval.
- Using grants to fund activities not permitted by the grant agreement.
- Promising future liquidity, distributions, yields, or return outcomes.

## Use-of-proceeds controls

### Board-approved budget process

For each raise, prepare a one-page **Use of Proceeds Schedule** with:

| Field | Required disclosure |
|---|---|
| Issuer | Exact legal entity receiving funds |
| Raise amount | Minimum, target, and maximum, if applicable |
| Security | SAFE, preferred equity, common equity, note, SPV interest, grant, or contract |
| Time horizon | Expected months of runway or project period |
| Approved budget categories | Product, payroll, content, equipment, sales, legal, reserves, etc. |
| Restricted uses | Items investors are not funding |
| Intercompany payments | Any planned payments to AGI affiliates and the governing agreement |
| Approval authority | Who can reallocate funds and at what threshold |
| Reporting cadence | Monthly management reporting; quarterly investor reporting |
| Material-deviation rule | When board or investor notice/approval is required |

### Example AGI Corp operating-company budget

| Use | Percentage | Guardrail |

| Product, AI, data, and engineering | 35%–45% | Prioritize core platform and customer-facing integrations |

## Investor reporting

### Quarterly investor report

Provide a consistent quarterly update to major equity investors:

| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | Major wins, misses, decisions, and next-quarter priorities |
| Cash and runway | Cash balance, burn rate, runway, financing plan |
| Revenue | ARR/MRR, contracted revenue, pipeline, churn/renewal, gross margin |
| Product | Releases, adoption, reliability, roadmap changes, security/accessibility status |
| Customers | Pilots, conversions, use cases, outcomes, retention, key risks |
| Team | Key hires, departures, open roles, advisors |
| Governance | Board decisions, related-party disclosures, material contracts, mission reporting |
| Risk register | New, elevated, mitigated, and unresolved risks |
| Ask | Introductions, customers, partners, expertise, hiring referrals |

### PBC impact report

At least annually — even if the formal statutory cadence is biennial — publish or share with stockholders:

- Public-benefit objectives.
- Defined impact metrics.
- Program outputs and outcomes.
- Accessibility and equitable-access measures.
- AI safety/governance activities.
- Fellowship, scholarship, and community-impact activity.
- Material incidents, lessons learned, and corrective actions.
- How the board balanced commercial, stakeholder, and public-benefit interests.

This aligns with the Delaware PBC reporting requirement to explain objectives, measurement standards, factual progress, and assessment of the corporation's public-benefit efforts.

## Final investor wording

Use this in the deck or offering memorandum:

> **Capital structure and governance.** AGI separates operating-company, robotics, education, real-estate, grant-funded, and public-benefit activities through distinct legal entities, budgets, and reporting. Each investor receives an interest only in the issuer identified in the applicable financing documents.
>
> AGI Future Foundation PBC protects a defined public-benefit mission through limited reserved rights over mission-critical actions. It does not direct routine commercial operations. AGI Corp and its subsidiaries operate under conventional board oversight, documented related-party agreements, and investor reporting standards.
>
> **Risk management.** Investments in early-stage AI, education, robotics, and real-estate technology involve substantial risk, including possible loss of the entire investment and lack of liquidity. AGI manages these risks through milestone-based capital deployment, phased product releases, simulation-first robotics strategy, human oversight of AI, standards-based interoperability, security/privacy controls, entity-level separation of capital, and transparent board and investor reporting.

The goal is not to make AGI appear risk-free. It is to show investors that you understand where risk lives, who owns each risk, how capital is protected from cross-entity confusion, and what objective milestones must be achieved before the company spends more capital or raises the next round.

| Item | Recommendation | Why this is the standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrument** | Y Combinator **post-money SAFE, valuation-cap only** — no discount, no MFN — one identical form for every investor in the round | The post-money SAFE (2018 form) is the most widely used pre-seed instrument; cap-only keeps the conversion math legible and avoids stacking incompatible terms |

| **Side letters** | YC **pro rata side letter** and information rights for investors at or above a threshold (e.g. $250K); nothing else | Limits special rights to the standard one; keeps the round closable |

| **Securities compliance** | **Regulation D, Rule 506(b)**: accredited investors, no general solicitation, subscription and accreditation process; **Form D within 15 days of first sale**; state blue-sky notice filings; controlled investor communications | The exemption most venture financings use; 506(b) permits no general solicitation, so decks and videos go to identified investors, not the public |
| **Mission rights** | A written Mission Rights Agreement: reserved matters limited to the public-benefit purpose, removal of safety/accessibility/privacy/oversight policies, sale or exclusive licence of mission-critical IP, dissolution, and related-party transactions above a threshold | Narrow enough to be financeable, specific enough to protect the charter |
| **Reporting** | Quarterly investor report (cash, runway, revenue, product, customers, team, governance, risk register, asks); annual public-benefit report — the statutory minimum is biennial (8 Del. C. §366) | Investors fund what they can see; the PBC statute requires the benefit report |
| **What not to do** | No uncapped SAFEs, no MFN stacking, no side-letter sprawl, no advisor equity without vesting and IP assignment, no promises of liquidity or returns, no "AGI" claims in offering materials | Each is a known diligence red flag |
