*A white paper from AGI Future Foundation PBC on the Third Place · Club Edition and the Club Administrator Academy in the Cognition.X Education OS (v0.71.0), written for Boys & Girls Clubs and organisations like them. September 2026.*

## 1. What this is, in one paragraph

A Club is not a school with different hours. It is a third place — the one between home and school — and almost everything that makes a school-day curriculum work is absent there: no roster, no age grade, no authority over homework, and a session run by somebody who is often nineteen, part-time, three shifts in, with no preparation time. Cognition.X's Education OS carries two modules built the other way up. **Third Place · Club Edition** is 225 self-contained, drop-in-safe, mixed-age sessions in five strands and three age bands, each shipped as a 3-2-1 card a new staff member can run from the wall, plus five summer intensives and a five-rung member ladder that ends in a real paid job. **The Club Administrator Academy** is 144 modules in twelve tracks and three tiers for the person promoted into a director's seat on a Friday, every module ending in an instrument the director keeps. This paper gives every session and every module, the needs assessment both were built from, the goals and how each is honestly measured, the five record types and what they must never hold, the refusals, and the partnership-readiness statement — which is, in every row, that nothing has been agreed with anybody.

## 2. What a Club actually needs

The assessment came first and the curriculum came out of it. Each row is a structural property of after-school youth development that a school-day product violates, and what this programme does instead.

| The need | What the design does |
|---|---|
| Drop-in attendance, not a roster | A nine-week block assumes continuity a Club cannot promise. Every session here is self-contained and order-independent, and progress accrues from whatever sessions a member actually attended rather than from a sequence they were supposed to complete. |
| Mixed ages in one room | Three bands rather than thirteen grades, and every session names its mixed-age move: what the older member does when a younger one is at the same table, so the answer is never "supervise them". |
| Staff who are part-time, new and untrained | Every session ships a cold-start card runnable in three minutes by somebody on their third shift with no preparation. If a session needs a trained facilitator, it says so on its face and does not pretend otherwise. |
| No authority over school work | A Club may not assign, grade, gate or report. The homework strand exists to make the school day survivable, never to become a second place a child is corrected. |
| Two hours after a full school day | Members arrive tired, hungry and already sat down for six hours. Movement and food come before anything requiring stillness, and nothing may feel like more school. |
| The relationship is the product | What a Club actually delivers is a caring adult who knows your name and a place you belong. Every session is scored on whether it created contact between a member and an adult, not on content covered. |
| Summer is a different programme | Full days for six to ten weeks, with the largest learning swing of the year inside it. The summer intensives are designed separately rather than being term-time stretched. |
| Reporting burden is already at capacity | Clubs already report attendance and outcomes to funders. This adds no new instrument: it emits attendance and session records in the shapes those reports already need, and nothing else. |
| Food, transport and the walk home | Whether a programme runs is decided by snack, a bus and daylight. These are first-class planning constraints here rather than logistics somebody else handles. |
| The teen cliff | Attendance falls off a cliff around thirteen. Teen programming is built as status, agency and paid responsibility rather than as supervised activity, because childcare is the one thing a fifteen-year-old will not attend. |
| Every Club is different | Some are in a school, some in public housing, some on a military installation with deployment cycles and constant moves, some on tribal land with sovereignty and language obligations. The programme ships as sessions to select from, never as a fixed year. |
| Ratios are not classroom ratios | One adult to twenty-five across three rooms is normal. Nothing here assumes a teacher-to-pupil ratio, and any session needing a tighter one states the number. |
| Members choose to be there | A Club member can leave. That is the whole design constraint of the sector, and it is a feature: a session nobody stays for has told you something a school-day evaluation would have hidden. |
| Safeguarding is the floor | Trained adults, visible spaces, two-adult rules, disclosure routes. Every session names the adult obligations it touches, and no session puts a teen in a position an adult should hold. |

## 3. The seven goals, and how each is measured

| Goal | What it means | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|
| **Belong** | Every member is known by name by at least two adults within a fortnight, and can name an adult they would go to. | By asking members, not by staff assertion. |
| **Be counted on** | Every member holds one real responsibility per term with a genuine consequence if dropped. | Recorded as a role held, not as a behaviour score. |
| **Make something** | Every member finishes and shows one thing per term to somebody who is not staff. | Counted as finished-and-shown, never juried. |
| **Keep school survivable** | The homework strand reduces the number of nights a member arrives at school with nothing done. | Self-reported by the member and never verified against a school. |
| **Move and eat** | Daily movement before stillness, and food that members helped make. | No weights, no measurements, no body data of any kind, ever. |
| **Earn and understand money** | Every Teen leaves with a real paid or costed experience and a documented record of it. | Evidence a member holds, not a file a Club holds about them. |
| **Hand it on** | Every Teen band member trains somebody behind them before they leave. | Observed once; not a requirement for recognition. |

## 4. The session shape — the 3-2-1 card

The whole programme is designed around one constraint: the person running the session is often nineteen, part-time, three shifts in and had no preparation time.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| The 3-2-1 card | Three minutes to start with no preparation, two things that must happen, one thing that must never. Printed, laminated, on the wall. A staff member on their third shift can run any session in this programme from the card alone. |
| Self-contained | No session requires the previous one. Arcs exist and are published, but every session stands up on a Tuesday a member has never been to before. |
| Mixed-age move | Every session names what the older member does. Never "help the little ones" — a specific role with a boundary. |
| Movement first | Anything requiring stillness is scheduled after movement, never before it, and movement is never withheld as a consequence. |
| Forty-five to seventy-five minutes | The real window between arrival, snack and pickup. Nothing here needs a double period. |
| Materials that exist | Every session lists materials a Club plausibly has, and a no-materials version. If it needs money, the amount is on the card. |
| Names the adult obligation | Safeguarding, allergy protocol, consent, ratio — whichever applies, stated at the top rather than assumed. |
| Ends with contact | Every session ends with one member and one adult exchanging something specific. That is the outcome; the content was the excuse. |

Every one of the 225 sessions prints as a card with the same shape: three minutes to start; the two things that must happen (the session's own, and *one member and one adult exchange something specific before the end — that is the outcome; the activity was the excuse*); the one thing that must never; what to do if a member arrives late (they join; nobody is caught up on anything); the mixed-age move; the adult obligations on the card (two adults or an open door; allergy protocol before any food; no photograph without written guardian consent; no body comment); and the status line: *Reference design. No Club has run this. Zero deployments.*

## 5. The curriculum — 225 sessions

Five strands × three bands × fifteen sessions. Every session is self-contained and order-independent; the codes are for the wall, not for a sequence.

### Homework & Learning Support

#### Homework & Learning Support · Juniors · 6–9

*Support the school day without owning it, and never let a Club become the place a child is corrected twice.*

| Code | Session | What happens | What it never is |
|---|---|---|---|
| HL-J-1 | The reading corner nobody is sent to | A member chooses a book and reads with an adult or an older member. Twenty minutes, no comprehension questions. | Never a punishment, never assigned, and never the place a member is sent for being loud. |
| HL-J-2 | Read to somebody smaller | An older member or an adult listens while the member reads aloud. The listener does not correct. | Never a fluency test. Nobody counts words per minute here. |
| HL-J-3 | The hard word wall | Members post one word that beat them today. Staff post one too. | Never a leaderboard, and never a member's name attached to a word without them putting it there. |
| HL-J-4 | Ten minutes on the thing that is due | Whatever is actually due tomorrow gets ten focused minutes with an adult within reach. | Never checked, never marked, never reported to a teacher. |
| HL-J-5 | Ask a better question | Practising the question that gets help: what part, what have I tried, what do I need. | Never used to make a member justify asking. |
| HL-J-6 | Maths with objects | Counting, grouping and fractions with real things — cups, cards, cash, cones. | Never a worksheet with a Club logo on it. |
| HL-J-7 | The five-minute win | One small thing finished completely, on purpose, and shown to one person. | Never extended into a longer task because it went well. |
| HL-J-8 | Reading the world | Signs, menus, bus times, game rules, packet labels — reading that is not a book. | Never framed as easier reading. |
| HL-J-9 | Two truths about a story | Retelling: what happened, and what you think about it. Both count. | Never a right answer for the second one. |
| HL-J-10 | The library card | Getting one, using one, and finding out what the local library actually gives away free. | Never a field trip that some members cannot join because of a fee. |
| HL-J-11 | Handwriting without shame | For members who find it physically hard. Grip, paper angle, breaks, and alternatives. | Never a neatness competition. |
| HL-J-12 | When school was hard today | A short, structured way to say it and be heard, then move on to something else. | Never a debrief that becomes an interrogation about a teacher. |
| HL-J-13 | Somebody else's homework | Helping without doing: the three sentences that help and the one that takes over. | Never a member finishing another member's work. |
| HL-J-14 | The finished-work shelf | A visible place where completed things sit for a week. | Never a display of grades or scores. |
| HL-J-15 | Show one grown-up | A member shows one adult one thing they did. That is the whole session. | Never a performance, never a presentation, never optional to have somebody there. |

## 8. What this programme refuses to do

| Refusal | In practice |
|---|---|
| **No Club becomes a second school** | No assigning, no grading, no gating, no reporting a member's work to a school without the member and their guardian choosing it. |
| **No body data** | No weight, height, BMI, body composition or comment on any member's body, plate or eating, at any age, by anyone, ever. |
| **No photograph of a member without written guardian consent** | Not for a funder report, not for social media, not for this platform. A consent on file is not a consent for every use. |
| **No teen used as unpaid staff** | Mentoring and helping are bounded and supervised. The moment a teen is covering a staffing gap, they are working, and working is paid. |
| **No new reporting instrument** | If a Club already reports it, this emits it in that shape. If a Club does not already report it, this does not start. |
| **No behaviour scoring** | No points, no charts with names, no colour systems, no public rankings of conduct. |
| **No recruitment of members into anything** | Not a faith, not a party, not a product, not a data set. A Club is a place to be, not a funnel. |

## 9. The Club Administrator Academy

Nobody trains the administrator. The sector promotes its best youth worker into a director's seat on a Friday and expects a building on Monday: safeguarding, ratios, a schedule for people who are students and parents, a budget written in a language nobody taught them, a funder report, an angry family, and a nineteen-year-old on their third shift. **The design promise:** every module ends in an instrument, not a takeaway. If a module does not hand you something you can put on a wall, run on a Tuesday or use to make one decision differently, it has failed and it should be cut.

Twelve tracks, three tiers, four modules each — 144 modules, about 76 hours. A tier is not a level of seniority you are awarded; it is where you currently are in a particular track, and a ten-year executive is routinely at tier one in a track they have never had to hold. No certificate is offered at the end of any of it.

### 9.1 The ten instruments

The training is only worth anything if the director keeps something. These are the ten screens and one-pagers the modules build toward, each named with what it deliberately is not, because the failure mode of every administrator tool in this sector is that it quietly becomes surveillance of staff.

| Instrument | What it does | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| The Monday board | One screen showing the week ahead: coverage gaps, ratio risk, members drifting, food, and the two decisions only the director can make. | A performance dashboard. Nothing on it is about individual staff quality. |
| The coverage grid | Rooms × hours × adults × ratios, with tomorrow's break highlighted before it happens. | A staff schedule with a colour scheme. |
| The ratio calculator | Enter members expected, adults rostered and rooms open; get the honest answer including which room closes. | A justification for running under ratio. |
| The drift read | Members whose attendance pattern changed, ordered by how recoverable it is, each with one named adult and one action. | A risk score attached to a child. |
| The teen-cliff watch | Your twelve-year-old cohort tracked forward, so the collapse at fourteen is a thing you saw coming. | A prediction about an individual member. |
| The ninety-second observation | A one-screen structured note from a doorway observation, with one specific piece of feedback due within the hour. | An evaluation record that follows a staff member. |
| The safeguarding walk | A weekly walk with the four failure modes named, producing a fix list rather than a compliance score. | An inspection. |
| The incident ladder | Five levels, each naming the call, the record and the timeline, so the response does not depend on how tired you are. | A decision tree that replaces your judgement. |
| The funder pack | Your existing records rendered in the shapes funders already ask for, with the gaps named rather than filled. | A report generator that invents outcomes. |
| The partnership one-pager | A working agreement template: who does what, on which days, whose insurance, whose data, how it ends. | A legal document. It is a starting point for a lawyer, not a substitute. |

### 9.2 The first ninety days in the seat

One plan for the person who was promoted on Friday. The order matters more than the content: three weeks of learning before a single change.

|  | Week | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Learn the building and the names | Open and close the site yourself every day. Learn forty member names. Do not change anything. |
| Week 2 | Learn the staff | A fifteen-minute one-to-one with every person, asking two questions: what is good here, and what would you fix if it were yours. |
| Week 3 | Learn the families | Stand at pickup every day. Five-minute conversations. First contact with ten families is a specific positive. |
| Week 4 | Read the records | Attendance for the last year, incidents, budget, funding obligations. Write down what you cannot yet explain. |
| Week 5 | The safeguarding walk | Your first full walk. Fix the physical things immediately; note the cultural ones. |
| Week 6 | Find the worst hour | Observe every hour of the week from the doorway. Name the one that is chaos. |
| Week 7 | Change one structural thing | Fix that hour by changing the room, the order, the food or the adult — not the rules. |
| Week 8 | Set the coverage grid | Build the grid. Find where the site breaks. Fix the schedule, not the people. |
| Week 9 | The drift read begins | Start the weekly fifteen-minute look at who is disappearing, with named adults and actions. |
| Week 10 | One partnership | Take the single most valuable external relationship and move it from coffee to a written arrangement. |
| Week 11 | Say the standard out loud | Name your three non-negotiables for a session, having now earned the right to. |
| Week 12 | Run a card cold, in front of everyone | Demonstrate the floor rather than describing it. |
| Week 13 | Tell your supervisor the truth | What is working, what is not, what you need, and what you got wrong in the first ninety days. |

### 9.3 The administrator ladder

| Rung | Seat | What you hold | How you know you are actually there |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Floor lead | You run sessions well and others follow how you do it. You hold a shift, not a site. | You are the person a new staff member is put with. |
| A2 | Site director | You hold a building: safety, staffing, the floor, the families, the day. | The site runs correctly on a day you are ill. |

| Record | What it holds | What it never holds |

### 9.6 What this training refuses to do

No certification is offered. No claim of effectiveness. No affiliation with any named organisation. No staff surveillance. No body data, on anybody. No behaviour record that travels. No partnership is implied.
