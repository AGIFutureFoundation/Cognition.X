# The Willie L. Brown Jr. Institute Track: a K–12 civic-leadership cornerstone, proposed

*A white paper from AGI Future Foundation PBC on the Institute Track model in the Cognition.X Education OS (v0.71.0), written for the Willie L. Brown Jr. Institute of Politics and Public Service. September 2026.*

> **Status and disclaimer.** This track is a proposal built from the Institute's public mission and the Willie L. Brown Jr. Fellowship Program at San Francisco State University. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or reviewed by the Institute, SF State, or Mayor Brown; the "Willie Brown Foundation" name in the brief refers to this Institute. A named track would be offered under a partnership agreement and the Institute's review of every course. Nothing in this paper uses the Institute's or Mayor Brown's name on a credential, and nothing presents the proposal as a partnership.

[TOC]

## 1. What this is, in one paragraph

Cognition.X is an open-source, offline-first learning platform whose Education OS carries fifty-six state editions of a K–12 curriculum. Inside it is a proposed civic-leadership cornerstone: the principles Willie L. Brown Jr. has taught through his Institute and his Fellowship — public service as the noblest profession, recruiting and including students of every background, hands-on learning inside real public agencies with a cohort and a mentor, breaking ceilings affirmatively, taking on the hopeless cases, tolerating the opposing view, transparency, bringing everyone along — turned into **33 strands, 3,333 courses and 33,333 modules** that a kindergartner can start and a twelfth-grader can carry into a Fellowship application, in every state edition, each course citing the principle it serves, every grade serving a real public agency at its own scale, a weekly seminar circle from grade 3, and evidence stacked toward the State Seal of Civic Engagement and the Institute's Fellowship. This paper sets out the principles and their sources, the architecture, every strand and bridge, every grade's courses, how the track is localised state by state, the legacy tool that keeps the Institute's principles teachable at scale, and what the track will never do without the Institute's agreement.

## 2. The Institute in the public record

The track is built only from what is public:

- **The vision statement** (wlbinstitute.org): *"Public service is the most noble of professions. It provides the opportunity to give back to your community, to ensure the next generation has opportunities to lead better lives."*
- **The mission:** education and training for emerging leaders while fostering civic engagement, leadership development and effective governance, with an emphasis on inclusivity and diversity.
- **The Fellowship** (SF State Institute for Civic and Community Engagement): 16 hours a week for 15 weeks inside a public agency, Friday seminars, a cohort focused on equity and social change, a stipend; designed to *"inspire and prepare SF State students from all majors to pursue a career in public service."*
- **The record:** 42 fellowships awarded by the time of the $1 million milestone (SF State News); four decades in California public life; the biography from Mineola, Texas (1934) to San Francisco (1951), SF State (1955) and Hastings (1958).

## 3. Twelve principles, with their sources

Each principle names the strands that carry it (the codes in section 5); every course cites the principles it serves.

| # | Principle (in the source's words) | Source | How the track teaches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Public service is the most noble of professions" | Institute vision statement (wlbinstitute.org) | Service is taught as a craft with roles, crews, evidence and credentials — not as a slogan. Every course ends with a real audience. |
| 2 | "Recruit and include students of diverse racial, cultural and economic backgrounds" | Willie L. Brown Jr., on the Fellowship (SF State News) | Who is in the room is a design decision. Every course asks who is missing and why; accommodation profiles, four-language signals and Tier 0 parity apply; barriers are named, not hidden. |
| 3 | "Hands-on learning inside real public agencies, with a cohort, a seminar and a stipend" | The Fellowship Program (SF State ICCE): 16 hours a week, 15 weeks, Friday seminars, cohort focused on equity and social change | A junior placement at every grade band and a weekly seminar circle from grade 3; in 11–12 a supervised, FLSA-compliant placement mirrors the Fellowship itself. |
| 4 | "Share knowledge and skills with a new generation of leaders" | Institute mission; Claremont McKenna Athenaeum description of Brown's mentoring role | Mentorship is a strand and a bridge: alumni and public servants as mentors and panelists; older students mentor younger cohorts; the leader's-story module in every course. |
| 5 | "Eagerly, aggressively and affirmatively push for those who have been blocked by the ceiling" | Willie L. Brown Jr., NPR interview on Basic Brown (2008) | Affirmative action, first-generation pathways and opportunity data are taught as history, law and practice — with the debate included, not avoided. |
| 6 | "Take on the cases others dismiss as hopeless" | Brown's early law career (Claremont McKenna Athenaeum) | Civil-rights law, housing discrimination (the 1960 sit-in), the Free Speech Movement defense: courses on standing up when the odds are long, with moot courts and rights cases to argue. |

## 4. The architecture

| Element | Count | How it is built |
|---|---:|---|
| Strands | 33 | Five families: service & governance · rights & justice · economy & place · health & wellbeing · equity & the person |
| Grades | 13 | Kindergarten through grade 12; every strand at every grade |
| Courses per strand per grade | 7 | 33 × 13 × 7 = 3,003 strand courses |
| Bridges | 11 | 30 courses each (two per grade K–12 plus family, summer, educator and community editions) = 330 |
| **Courses** | **3,333** | 3,003 + 330 |
| Modules per course | 10 | M1 Brief → M2 Leader's story → M3 Investigate → M4 Field → M5 Build → M6 Seminar circle → M7 Verify → M8 Present → M9 Reflect → M10 Credential |
| Track-wide modules | 3 | Track Foundations · Civic Safety, Ethics & Privacy · Fellowship Bridge |
| **Modules** | **33,333** | 3,333 × 10 + 3 |
| State editions | 56 | 50 states, DC, and the territories; 186,648 courses and 1,866,648 modules nationally |

**The placement ladder** runs through every grade: K–2 the classroom council; 3–5 the school and the county seat; 6–8 the neighborhood, city hall and the county; 9–10 a public agency project (county, city or state); 11–12 a supervised placement with a public agency, mirroring the Fellowship itself and compliant with child-labor law. **The seminar circle** — who decided, who was left out, what would you change — runs weekly from grade 3.

Every course ends with a real audience, and every credential the platform issues names the strand and the witnessed work — never the Institute's name, and never a title the credential cannot confer.

## 8. The legacy tool

The page's legacy tool states what the track is for and what it is not:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fellows since 2008** | 42 fellowships by the $1M milestone (SF State News); the K–12 track is designed to feed the pipeline by the hundreds |
| **Funds raised** | More than $1 million for the Fellowship — a model for the endowment the K–12 track would carry in each state |
| **What the legacy tool does** | Keeps the Institute's principles teachable at scale: every course cites the principle it serves; every credential names the strand; every state edition carries the origin story; the alumni network is a bridge, not a footnote |
| **What it never does** | Speaks for the Institute or Mayor Brown, uses their names on a credential without agreement, or presents a proposal as a partnership |

**Legacy pipeline — targets, not results:** junior fellows (grade 8 bridge) per state per year, 1,000; seminar circles running weekly, every school on the track; civic-seal portfolios sealed per year, one per twelfth-grader on the track; Fellowship on-ramp applications (California), a multiple of the 42 fellows of the first decade; alumni and public-servant mentors on the network, one per crew; state editions carrying the cornerstone, 51.

## 9. What a partnership would and would not be

- **Would be:** a partnership agreement; the Institute's review of every course before it carries the name; the Fellowship on-ramp mapped to the Institute's actual eligibility; alumni invited as mentors and panelists on their own terms; the endowment model offered, not assumed.
- **Would not be:** any use of the Institute's or Mayor Brown's name on a credential, a deck, a grant or a press line before agreement; any claim that the Institute has reviewed, endorsed or adopted the track; any data about a student leaving the platform without that student's and their guardian's consent.
- **How to start:** read the track in the Education OS (*Willie L. Brown Jr. Institute Track*, any state edition), export any grade as JSON, and say what is wrong with it. The platform corrects its proposal before it asks for anything.

## 11. Appendix — the platform behind the page

- **Open source:** github.com/AGIFutureFoundation/Cognition.X. Curriculum content is CC BY 4.0.
- **Offline, single file:** each app is one HTML file that opens from a USB stick; no account, no server, no telemetry, no third-party script; a strict Content-Security-Policy enforced by the browser.
- **Records:** a credential is issued at exactly fifty witnessed checks, signed offline and verifiable without the platform; Open Badges 3.0 / W3C Verifiable Credential envelopes available. A simulation is practice, never a check.
- **Accessibility:** audited at WCAG 2.2 AA (47 views, zero WCAG-tagged violations, keyboard sweep clean).
- **Honesty:** every generated document is rebuilt in CI and fails on drift; every page that names the Institute carries the disclaimer above, verbatim.
