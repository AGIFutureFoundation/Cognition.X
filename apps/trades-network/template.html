The trades, the halls, and the classroom between them

A regional training network on two working coasts — San Francisco, Oakland–East Bay and Los Angeles on the Pacific; New Orleans, Baton Rouge–River Region and Houston–Gulf Coast on the Gulf: 37 trade families × 6 regions = 222 union & trade entries, each with a training simulation set on its region's own ground, curriculum packs behind it, and a flipped, gamified classroom model that brings the trades into school districts on honest terms.

How a learner moves through it

  1. Flipped lesson — the concept travels home as a short watch; the classroom hours go to the bench.
  2. Simulation studio — control discipline on a scenario set at the region's own port, grid, yard or pump station.
  3. Bench & transfer check — hands on real material, witnessed and recorded in the learner's ledger.
  4. The hall — mentor hours, a training-center visit, and the apprenticeship pathway found through the council.
  5. The pathway is the learner's — a bench record travels to a hall only when the learner sends it.

The flipped, gamified classroom

The district integration model: lecture moves home as a seven-minute watch, the teacher's live hours go to the bench, and an honest game layer — quests, guilds, points that map one-to-one onto passed transfer checks — keeps the room in the flow channel.

The flipped cycle

The game layer, honestly

  1. Points mean blocks — every XP maps to a passed transfer check; a point that can be earned without competence kills the currency.
  2. Guilds are crews — four learners, rotating roles (lead, safety, scribe, quartermaster), because the trades work in crews.
  3. The boss fight is the transfer check — the unit's climax is its assessment, staged with stakes and a witness.
  4. Failure costs a retry, not a record — priced in time and another attempt, never carried as a mark.
  5. Badges are credentials in costume — the badge set maps onto Explorer → Builder → Practitioner → Lead exactly.

      The district compact

      Working with school districts

      Bringing the trades onto the transcript — the compact between a district and its regional labor council, with the flipped model as the classroom engine and the network roster as the pathway map.

      The compact in six commitments

      1. The board memo — the case in the district's own terms: engagement, completion, pathways to wages.
      2. Credit where credit is due — bench hours mapped to CTE and elective credit; the credential ladder mapped to the district's units.
      3. The classroom becomes a studio — facilities surveyed honestly; the studio law posted; counsel reads the permission packet.
      4. Mentors through the council — tradespeople in the room and hall visits, arranged through the regional council, never a cold call to a local.
      5. The five honest numbers — enrolled, benched, checked, credentialed, placed-into-pathway; reported to the board unvarnished.
      6. Equity is a roster question — the course roster read against the school's population shape every term, with the outreach step named.
